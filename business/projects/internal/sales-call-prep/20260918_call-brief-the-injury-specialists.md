## 30-Second Snapshot
The Injury Specialists is a 20-year-old (founded 2006) B2B referral network connecting physicians, chiropractors/PT, attorneys, and imaging centers around personal injury and workers' comp cases — 1,100+ vetted professionals across 40+ states. Revenue is membership/dues-based: professionals pay to join a proprietary web portal for referrals, medical-records retrieval, and networking, each with a dedicated Account Manager. It's founder-led and doctor-owned, with Michael Wax as co-founder alongside Dr. Golden. The most likely reason to take this call: a two-sided referral network's whole value proposition rides on manual, people-heavy back-office work (vetting, records retrieval, account management) that hasn't changed much since the platform's original build, while the company is actively pushing into new markets and content marketing to grow.

## Patterns to Probe (from past engagements)
1. **Already built, never adopted** — This company has a genuinely proprietary, two-decades-old tech platform, but there's no visible sign of ongoing product investment (WordPress site, outside agency, copyright frozen at 2021). Members may not be using every feature the platform already has. *Proof story:* "A lending client had an entire sales follow-up system already built into their CRM. Nobody used it. The biggest win wasn't a build, it was adoption."
2. **Growth capped by follow-up, not lead flow** — In a referral network, growth is capped less by member interest and more by how fast vetting, onboarding, and account management can absorb new members without breaking. *Proof story:* "Growth wasn't capped by leads coming in. It was capped by what happened after a lead landed."
3. **Files nobody trusts** — Medical records move across 1,100+ independent practices with real HIPAA stakes; if organization and retrieval are inconsistent, that's exactly the kind of gap that becomes dangerous once AI starts touching those files directly. *Proof story:* "As AI starts working inside your files, how they're organized decides whether you get the right answer or a confident wrong one."
