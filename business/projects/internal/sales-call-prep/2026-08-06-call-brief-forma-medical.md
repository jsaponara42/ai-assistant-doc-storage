## Anticipated Pains
1. **Surgeon education & onboarding friction** — Each new technique (especially MIS approaches) requires hands-on training. Surgeons adopt at different paces. Following up on trial kits and getting adoption data is manual and slow.
2. **Multi-vendor supplier coordination** — Multiple implant components need sourcing, quality control, and on-time delivery. Managing different suppliers, lead times, and quality specs is complex, especially when launching new products.
3. **Demand forecasting & production scheduling** — Four product lines now. Early adopter data is sparse. How much OptimalTMT to produce? Can't overstock but can't stock out. Misalignment between sales forecast and manufacturing leads to delays or waste.
4. **Regulatory & quality documentation at scale** — FDA compliance, device history records, and traceability are mandatory. Managing documentation across four product lines, multiple SKUs, and growing surgical volume is labor-intensive if done manually.
5. **Sales team coordination & lead visibility** — Hiring new regional sales directors. Without a shared system, duplicative outreach, gaps in follow-up, and no clear handoff between who's qualified, who's in trial, and who's a closed account.

---

## Sharp Questions to Ask
- How are you currently managing surgeon education and onboarding when adopting new techniques like OptimalMTP?
- With four product lines now live, how are you forecasting demand and coordinating production without under- or over-producing?
- As you grow the sales team, how are you preventing follow-up gaps and ensuring each surgeon is tracked consistently?
- What's the biggest bottleneck right now between product development and getting devices into surgeons' hands?
- How are you managing regulatory documentation and device traceability across your expanding portfolio?
