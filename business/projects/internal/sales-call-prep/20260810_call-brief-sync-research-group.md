# Call Brief — Sync Research Group
**Date:** 
**Industry:** Market research operations / qualitative research support

---

## The Company
- **What they do:** Boutique research operations support for market research and insights teams — participant recruitment, field logistics, project management, transcription, and translation.
- **Size:** Small / boutique (est. 6-15 core team, likely supplemented by a contractor network for transcription and translation)
- **Stage:** Established — 9+ years in operation, rebranded from SyncScript to Sync Research Group as services expanded
- **Tech signals:** Own participant panel platform (Opinions by Sync), video verification for recruitment screening, markets itself as offering "AI-powered insights"
- **Notable:** Positions against both expensive high-touch firms and cheap low-quality ones — value prop is personalized, affordable quality; builds a per-client preference library manually after each first project

- **Tenure:** Long-tenured, on since the SyncScript era

- **Notable:** Based in NH, remote from Liz in PA — a distributed team; colleagues describe her as detail-oriented

---

## Anticipated Pains
1. Client preferences and project history are tracked as a manual "library" built after each first project — likely docs or spreadsheets, not a structured system, and inconsistent as volume grows
2. Participant recruitment and screening (including video verification) is coordinated manually per project — scheduling, no-show replacement, and confirmation likely eat significant PM time
3. Transcription and translation QA is a manual proofing step even where AI tools generate a first pass — accuracy claims depend on human review that doesn't scale linearly with volume
4. Project managers juggle many concurrent client projects, each with unique specs, without a shared automated status/reporting system — bottlenecks surface as volume grows
5. Their core differentiator — personalized, high-touch service at an affordable price — gets structurally harder to sustain profitably as they scale without automation absorbing the routine work

---

## Sharp Questions to Ask
- "How are you tracking each client's preferences and project history today — is that in someone's head, a doc, or a system?"
- "On a typical multi-project week, where does most of a PM's time actually go — coordination, or the deliverable work itself?"
- "How much manual QA and proofing goes into a transcript or translation before it goes out the door?"
- "What's taking the most time in your operation right now that you wish wasn't?"
- "You mention AI-powered insights on the site — what's actually live today versus aspirational?"
- To Trish: "As you've scaled the ops side, where do the seams start to show first — recruitment, scheduling, QA, or reporting?"

---

## What to Listen For
A strong signal is Liz or Trish framing AI as a way to protect and scale the personal-touch service they've built their reputation on, rather than replace it — and Trish naming a specific operational seam (recruitment, QA, reporting) unprompted rather than speaking only in generalities.
