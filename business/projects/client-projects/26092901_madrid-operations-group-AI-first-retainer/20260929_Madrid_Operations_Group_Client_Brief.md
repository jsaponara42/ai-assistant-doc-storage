## 7. Problem Register

> **Provisional.** These are inferred from Martina's behavior and role across three Blue Tusk engagements, not diagnosed in a discovery session with her. Each needs confirming before it goes in the SOW.

### P-001 — Rebuilding the same operational assets per client *(inferred)*
- **Area:** Processes / Resources.
- **Problem:** Martina produces similar work at every client — SOPs, AI governance and gripe-list setup, CRM cleanup, vendor coordination — largely from scratch inside each client's tools. Blue Tusk has already seen the pattern: the RFG AI Playbook was adapted from the Maycomb Playbook.
- **Current Thinking:** Each client is bespoke, so each deliverable is built for that client.
- **Reframe:** Most of the structure repeats; only the specifics change. A reusable library of templates, prompts, and Claude skills turns each new client into a configuration job rather than a build.
- **Approach:** Inventory what she rebuilds per client; convert the top recurring assets into reusable templates and Claude skills/projects owned by Madrid Ops. Status: Not started.

### P-002 — Carrying the AI-owner role at multiple clients by hand *(inferred)*
- **Area:** People.
- **Problem:** Martina holds AI ownership or admin at Maycomb (joint AI owner, enforcement authority) and RFG (Claude admin, joint "who runs this"), plus automation ownership at LFG. Each role involves monitoring gripe lists, answering "is this OK to try" questions, and keeping governance current — on top of her COO scope.
- **Current Thinking:** Owning AI adoption is part of the fractional COO job; she can absorb it.
- **Reframe:** Owning it and running it are different. With a shared playbook, a shared cadence, and Blue Tusk behind her, the role becomes a repeatable service she delivers efficiently — and potentially bills for — rather than unpaid overhead at each client.
- **Approach:** Standardize one AI-steward operating rhythm she can run across all clients, with Blue Tusk as backstop. Status: Not started.

### P-003 — Context fragmented across client tenants and identities *(inferred)*
- **Area:** Systems / Tools.
- **Problem:** Four email identities, separate Claude seats, and separate document systems per client. Her own notes, decisions, and open items for each client likely live in several places, making context-switching costly and AI assistance hard to point at one coherent body of knowledge.
- **Current Thinking:** Separation per client is required for confidentiality, so fragmentation is the cost of doing business.
- **Reframe:** Confidentiality requires separating client *data*, not scattering Martina's own operating knowledge. A single, well-structured Madrid Ops knowledge base (her notes, templates, per-client status, decisions) can sit cleanly apart from each client's data.
- **Approach:** Design a Madrid Ops workspace structure with clear per-client boundaries and rules for what never leaves a client tenant. Status: Not started.

### P-004 — No AI usage policy for the practice itself *(inferred)*
- **Area:** People / Tools.
- **Problem:** Martina helps set AI policy for her clients but there's no record of Madrid Ops having its own. Clients differ sharply (LFG bans financial data in AI; Maycomb handles confidential fund and investor data; RFG gates Cowork on governance). An AI-first practice handling all three needs one rule set that respects the strictest client.
- **Current Thinking:** Each client's policy governs its own data, so a practice-level policy isn't needed.
- **Reframe:** Her practice is the point where all three clients' data could cross. A practice-level policy is what lets her go AI-first without breaching any client's terms.
- **Approach:** Short Madrid Ops AI usage policy, adapted from the Maycomb/RFG playbooks, with per-client data-handling rules. Status: Not started.

### P-005 — Practice growth capped by her hours *(inferred)*
- **Area:** Resources.
- **Problem:** A solo fractional practice scales linearly with Martina's time. Every new client adds a full seat of hours.
- **Current Thinking:** More clients means more hours, or hiring.
- **Reframe:** Her own thesis — you need smarter structure, not more hours — applied to her own business. AI-first delivery raises how many clients she can serve well.
- **Approach:** Depends on P-001 through P-004. Status: Not started.

## 8. Action Plan

**Plan Overview:** Five provisional problems, all pointing at the same constraint: Martina's practice is built on her personal time and repeated per-client effort, while she increasingly carries AI ownership inside each client. The likely top priority is capacity relief. Sequencing logic: policy and structure first (because her practice sits where three clients' confidential data could cross), then reusable assets and a standard AI-steward rhythm, then growth. Everything here is provisional until a kickoff/discovery session with Martina confirms the problems and priorities.

### People
**Problems in scope:** P-002, P-004

**Workstream: Madrid Ops AI Usage Policy**
- **Approach:** Adapt the Maycomb and RFG playbooks into a practice-level policy.
- **What it involves:** Per-client data-handling rules (strictest-client default); which Claude account is used for what; what never leaves a client tenant.
- **Key Result:** A one-to-two page policy Martina operates under and can show clients.
- **Timeline:** Week 1–2. No dependencies.

**Workstream: Standard AI-Steward Rhythm**
- **Approach:** One repeatable operating cadence for the AI-owner role she holds at multiple clients.
- **What it involves:** Common gripe-list format, triage checklist, monthly review template, "is this OK to try" decision guide; Blue Tusk as escalation backstop.
- **Key Result:** Same rhythm running at Maycomb and RFG, measurably less time per client.
- **Timeline:** Weeks 2–5, after the policy.

### Systems / Tools
**Problems in scope:** P-003

**Workstream: Madrid Ops Workspace Structure**
- **Approach:** One home for Martina's own operating knowledge, cleanly separated from client data.
- **What it involves:** Folder/knowledge-base taxonomy, per-client status pages, naming conventions, Claude project setup against it.
- **Key Result:** Martina can get up to speed on any client in minutes and point Claude at her own knowledge safely.
- **Timeline:** Weeks 1–4, concurrent with the policy.

### Processes / Resources
**Problems in scope:** P-001, P-005

**Workstream: Reusable Asset Library**
- **Approach:** Convert repeat deliverables into templates, prompts, and Claude skills.
- **What it involves:** Inventory recurring work across clients; build the top three to five as reusable assets (e.g., SOP-from-walkthrough, AI governance playbook, gripe-list setup, client onboarding checklist).
- **Key Result:** New-client onboarding and common deliverables built from the library, not from scratch.
- **Timeline:** Weeks 3–8, after the workspace structure exists.

**Workstream: AI-First Practice Growth**
- **Approach:** Use the library and steward rhythm to take on more clients or package AI operations as part of her offer.
- **What it involves:** Define what Madrid Ops can now offer; optionally formalize the Blue Tusk referral/partner relationship.
- **Key Result:** Capacity for more clients without proportionally more hours.
- **Timeline:** Ongoing, after the library is in use.

### Recommended Sequence
1. Kickoff/discovery session with Martina — confirm problems, priorities, and her own stack (prerequisite to everything).
2. Madrid Ops AI Usage Policy — no dependencies.
3. Madrid Ops Workspace Structure — concurrent with #2.
4. Standard AI-Steward Rhythm — after #2.
5. Reusable Asset Library — after #3.
6. AI-First Practice Growth — after #4 and #5 are in use.
