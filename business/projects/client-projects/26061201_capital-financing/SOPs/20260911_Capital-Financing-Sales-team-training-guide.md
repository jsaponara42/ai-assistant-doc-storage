Capital Financing was launched in 2010 based on direct feedback from attorneys who were frustrated with their experiences dealing with pre-settlement funding companies. These attorneys saw an industry full of hidden fees, confusing contracts, and a lack of transparency — and they wanted something better.

Out of that frustration, Capital Financing was built from the ground up with one goal: to be the most client-friendly financial services company in the legal funding industry. Today we provide two core services — Pre-Settlement Advances to injured victims and Case Expense Financing to law firms — operating in over 30 states.

Section 3: Our Services — Pre-Settlement Advances

What Is a Pre-Settlement Advance?

A pre-settlement advance provides injured victims with immediate access to funds while their case is still pending. The advance is repaid from settlement proceeds — the client pays nothing out of pocket upfront. When a law firm trusts Capital Financing, they direct their clients to us rather than letting them search online and risk landing with a company that has hidden fees or poor service.

  

Our Pre-Settlement Differentiators

**Fee Model & Transparency**

- Simple fee model — not interest-based, no compounding
- Fees cap at 12 months — clients never owe more after month 12 (certain case types may not qualify)
- Full Disclosure Statement on Page 1 — payment schedules, fees, amount borrowed, and net client payout all visible upfront
- Competitors often bury disclosure statements to obscure true costs — Capital Financing puts everything on page 1

  

**Process & Ease of Use**

- Little required of the law firm (2 mins of their time)-Focused on plaintiff
- Same-day approval in most cases (all depends on the plaintiff and/or law firm)
- We review every contract with the plaintiff to ensure they are comfortable — most companies do not do this
- Multiple delivery options: ACH/Wire, Debit Card, Overnight FedEx (only $25)
- Pre-Settlement will most of the time take precedence over Case Expense reviews(discuss)

  

**Background Checks**

- We conduct background checks on every client: Criminal, Child Support, and Bankruptcy

This protects law firms from unknowingly referring clients with outstanding obligations that could complicate settlement. Most competitors do not offer this.  Why is this important? 

  

**Settlement & Negotiation**

- Because fees terminate at 12 months*, clients typically owe less than with competitors — less negotiation needed at settlement

Section 12: Tort Reform & The Changing Legal Landscape

Why This Matters to Every FC

Tort reform is reshaping the personal injury legal landscape across the United States — and it is one of the most powerful conversation topics an FC can bring to a law firm. When you understand what is happening in the industry and can speak intelligently about it, you position yourself not as a vendor, but as an informed strategic partner who understands their world.

This section provides an overview of tort reform, its specific impacts on Personal Injury law firms, and how Capital Financing's solutions are uniquely positioned to help firms navigate this new reality. Note that this is a living, evolving topic — deeper discussion and current developments will be covered in live training sessions.

  

What Is Tort Reform?

Tort reform refers to legislative and regulatory changes designed to limit civil lawsuits and reduce the size of damage awards — particularly in personal injury cases. Driven largely by insurance companies and corporate interests, tort reform efforts have been sweeping through state legislatures across the country, fundamentally changing the environment in which PI law firms operate.

Key elements of tort reform include:

- Caps on non-economic damages — limiting what plaintiffs can recover for pain and suffering
- Shorter statutes of limitations — reducing the window for filing claims
- Changes to comparative negligence rules — making it harder for plaintiffs to recover if they bear any share of fault
- Limitations on attorney fees — directly impacting law firm revenue on contingency cases
- Increased filing requirements and procedural hurdles — raising the cost and complexity of litigation
- Medical damage limitations — capping recoverable medical expenses at amounts paid rather than billed

  

How Tort Reform Is Impacting PI Law Firms

|   |
|---|
|**_Tort reform is a David vs. Goliath story. Law firms are being asked to fight harder, longer, and at greater cost — against insurance companies with virtually unlimited resources._**|

  

**Longer Case Timelines**

Tort reform has introduced more procedural complexity into litigation, meaning cases that once settled in 12-18 months may now take 3-5 years or longer. For law firms operating on contingency, this means capital is tied up for much longer periods — creating serious cash flow pressure and limiting the firm's ability to grow or take on new cases.

  

**Higher Litigation Costs**

Fighting back against well-funded insurance defense teams requires investment — expert witnesses, life care planners, medical specialists, depositions, and more. As tort reform raises the bar for what it takes to win a case, the cost of building a strong case goes up significantly. Firms that cannot fund these expenses at the level needed are at a disadvantage before they ever reach a courtroom.

  

**Increased Pressure to Settle Early**

When law firms are stretched thin financially — with capital locked up in cases and operating expenses mounting — there is enormous pressure to settle cases before their full value is realized. Tort reform, by extending timelines and increasing costs, intensifies this pressure. Firms may settle for less than a case is worth simply because they cannot afford to wait.

  

**Impact on Plaintiff Financial Stability**

Injured plaintiffs waiting years for their cases to resolve are often in financial distress — unable to work, facing mounting medical bills, and without income. When plaintiffs cannot sustain themselves financially through the litigation process, they pressure their attorneys to settle early or may disengage entirely. This directly impacts case outcomes and firm revenue.

  

**Increased Volume of Filings**

Paradoxically, some tort reform measures have created urgency among plaintiffs and attorneys to file cases before new restrictions take effect, leading to increased case volumes in certain markets. More cases mean more demand for both pre-settlement funding and case expense financing.

  

How Capital Financing Addresses Each Impact

|   |   |
|---|---|
|**Tort Reform Impact**|**How Capital Financing Helps**|
|**Longer case timelines — capital tied up for years**|Case Expense Financing frees up firm capital so the firm can continue operating and growing regardless of how long cases take to resolve.|
|**Higher litigation costs — expert fees, depositions, specialists**|Capital Financing pays vendor invoices directly or reimburses the firm — enabling firms to invest fully in building the strongest possible case.|
|**Pressure to settle early below case value**|By removing cash flow pressure, Capital Financing allows firms to hold cases longer and maximize settlement value for both the client and the firm.|
|**Plaintiff financial distress during long litigation**|Pre-Settlement Advances give plaintiffs the financial stability to stay the course — reducing pressure on both the client and the firm to settle prematurely.|
|**Smaller firms competing against large insurers**|Capital Financing levels the playing field — giving smaller and growing firms access to capital that allows them to litigate as aggressively as firms with far greater resources.|

  

Using Tort Reform in Your Conversations

Tort reform is not just background knowledge — it is a conversation tool. When you bring up tort reform with a law firm, you demonstrate that you understand their world at a deeper level than any other funding company rep walking through their door. It opens doors to conversations about case timelines, cash flow pressure, and litigation investment that naturally lead to Capital Financing's solutions.

Section 13: Operations Overview — From Inception to Settlement

Why Financial Consultants Need to Know This

You are the face of Capital Financing to every law firm you work with. When a firm refers a client or submits a case expense request, they will have questions about what happens next. Understanding the full operational journey allows you to set accurate expectations, communicate confidently, and strengthen trust with your law firm partners.

You do not need to operate these departments. You need to understand them well enough to represent them accurately and direct law firm questions to the right person.

  

The Journey at a Glance

|   |   |   |   |   |   |   |   |   |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|**1**<br><br>**Intake**|**→**|**2**<br><br>**Underwriting**|**→**|**3**<br><br>**Contracting**|**→**|**4**<br><br>**Deploying**|**→**|**5**<br><br>**Servicing**|

  

Department 1: Intake

|   |
|---|
|**_Intake is the front line — the first point of contact for every plaintiff and every law firm that reaches out to Capital Financing._**|

The Intake department handles all incoming requests for both B2C (plaintiffs seeking pre-settlement advances) and B2B (law firms seeking case expense financing). They receive inquiries via phone and email.

  

**For Pre-Settlement Advances**

- Collects as much information as possible directly from the plaintiff — injuries, case details, and all available background
- Reaches out to the law firm with a templated email of 4-5 targeted questions needed to complete intake and send the case to Underwriting
- Performs background checks on every plaintiff — Criminal, Child Support, and Bankruptcy — and conducts searches on any new law firm
- Creates an Injured Party Request (IPR) in Salesforce, triggering automated communications including an alert to the referring FC, and automated emails and texts to the plaintiff that continue through each department
- Requests any additional documentation needed before passing the case to Underwriting

  

**For Case Expense Financing**

- All communication is with the law firm only — no direct communication with the plaintiff
- The law firm provides all required information directly to Intake
- Intake may request additional documentation before forwarding to Underwriting

  

**What This Means for You as an FC**

When a law firm's client contacts Capital Financing, you will receive an automated Salesforce alert when the IPR is created. Use this as an opportunity to proactively reach out to the law firm to acknowledge the referral. Your involvement does not end when the referral is made — stay engaged.

Intake is overseen by Christy Imbriale, Director of Operations. All law firm questions about the intake process should be directed to Christy.

Department 2: Underwriting

|   |
|---|
|**_Underwriting evaluates every case and makes the approval or denial decision based on the merits of the claim._**|

Once Intake completes its process, Underwriting reviews all information and makes a funding decision. Underwriting does not communicate with the plaintiff by phone — all verbal communication is with the law firm directly, either with a paralegal or attorney.

  

**Approvals**

- When a case is approved, Underwriting updates Salesforce triggering automated approval emails and texts to the plaintiff
- The case moves forward to Contracting

  

**Denials**

- When a case is denied, Underwriting updates both Salesforce and Mighty with the denial
- The specific reason for the denial is recorded in Mighty — check here to understand why a client was declined
- Automated denial emails and texts are sent to the plaintiff

  

**What This Means for You as an FC**

When a client is denied, check Mighty for the reason, then proactively call the law firm to explain what happened. Transparency and follow-through at this stage builds trust. Underwriting is also overseen by Christy Imbriale — direct any law firm questions her way.

Department 3: Contracting

|   |
|---|
|**_Contracting ensures every agreement is fully understood, properly executed, and protecting all parties before any money moves._**|

  

**For Pre-Settlement Advances**

- Conducts a detailed review of the Disclosure Statement with the plaintiff so they fully understand all terms
- Determines how the plaintiff would like to receive their funds
- Sends contract to the plaintiff for signature and provides a copy to the law firm for their signature

  

**For Case Expense Financing**

- All communication is with the law firm only
- Collects banking information for reimbursements or direct vendor payments
- Law firm signs both the contract and an Addendum document specific to case expense financing
- Confirms retainer agreement language is in order before proceeding

  

**What This Means for You as an FC**

The Contracting stage is where delays often occur. If a case is stalling in contracting, a friendly outreach from you to the law firm contact can help move things along. Contracting is managed by Yasmine Swain, Director of Contracting, Funding & Servicing.

Department 4: Deploying Money

|   |
|---|
|**_This is the moment the client or law firm receives their funds — the payoff of everything that came before it._**|

  

**For Pre-Settlement Advances**

- Funds delivered via the plaintiff's chosen method: ACH/wire, debit card, or overnight FedEx ($25)
- Delivery is fast — same day or next day in most cases once contracts are complete

  

**For Case Expense Financing**

- Vertical 1 (Reimburse Firm): funds wired directly to the law firm's bank account
- Vertical 2 (Pay Vendors): payment sent directly to the third-party vendor

  

**What This Means for You as an FC**

Deployment is a great moment to reinforce the relationship. A quick message to the law firm confirming funds were delivered demonstrates that Capital Financing delivers on its promises — and is a natural opening to ask for the next referral.

Department 5: Servicing & Settlement

|   |
|---|
|**_Servicing manages every active case from the moment funds are deployed through to the final payoff at settlement._**|

  

**Ongoing Case Management**

- Sets up follow-up tasks to contact the law firm every 90 days
- Follow-up cadence can be customized based on the firm's preferences
- Sends payoff balance to the law firm every 90 days along with a simple case status update

  

**Settlement & Payoff**

- When a case settles, the law firm requests a payoff balance from Servicing
- Servicing provides the payoff amount before payment is made
- Any requests for fee reductions are escalated to the CEO for approval — no reductions made without CEO sign-off

  

**Special Circumstances**

- Terminated or dropped cases — handled by Servicing
- Cases where Capital Financing was not paid — investigated and resolved by Servicing
- Errors or mistakes that occurred during the process — corrected and documented in Servicing

  

**What This Means for You as an FC**

If a law firm contacts you with questions about a payoff balance, case status, or fee reduction, direct them to Yasmine Swain. When you know a case is approaching settlement, proactively reach out to reinforce the relationship and position yourself for the next referral.
