# Department Deep-Dive 1:1 Call Prep — Christy, Danielle, Yasmine

## Summary

Prep questionnaires for the three individual 1-hour follow-up calls (post-reset) with Christy, Danielle, and Yasmine. Each call runs **45 minutes of listening** — sharp questions that show JC already understands the shape of the department, but genuinely wants them to explain it in their own words — followed by **15 minutes re-training on the Easy SOP dictation method**. One document, three questionnaires, shared closing script.

## Context

Resets are done and landed well. This is the next layer down: understanding what each department is actually responsible for and how specific workflows run inside it, department by department, in their own words rather than reconstructed from the workflow map and discovery brief. Source grounding for the "shows understanding" framing in each opener: [[20260616-Workflow-Map-Capital-Financing-merged]] and [[20260612_discovery-brief-capital-financing]]. The Easy SOP segment reuses the method from the July all-teams session — see [[20260714_Capital-Financing_SOP-Call-Prep_All-Teams]] — condensed for a 1:1 refresher rather than a first introduction.

## Content

### How to use this doc, live

- **Don't read questions verbatim.** These are a bank to pull from — lead with the "Primary" questions, use "Follow-ups" only where the answer leaves something unclear or thin.
- **Let silence sit.** The instruction is to listen for 45 minutes — resist the urge to fill gaps with JC's own read of the workflow map. If they confirm something already documented, a quick "got it, matches what I had" is enough; don't over-explain what's already known.
- **Watch the clock loosely, not rigidly.** 45/15 is the target shape, not a hard cutoff — better to let a genuinely useful thread run 5 minutes long than to cut someone off mid-explanation.
- **Every opener below states something JC already believes to be true, then explicitly invites correction.** This is deliberate — it shows preparation without assuming the documented version is right. If they correct it, that correction is itself valuable data (several things in the workflow map are still `[TO CONFIRM]`).

## 1. Christy Imbriale — Director of Operations & Sr. Underwriter

### Opening frame (say this first)

*"From what I've pieced together, you're the anchor for underwriting — you personally vet every case, especially anything complex or high-dollar, and you don't take an attorney's version of a case at face value because they're financially incentivized to oversell it. You also oversee the intake team and the two VAs who do document collection. And on top of your own underwriting load, Howie's asked you to be the one I work through to get direction out to the rest of the team. I want to make sure I actually understand how all of that works day to day, in your words — tell me what you'd say your department is responsible for, and I'll ask questions as we go."*

### Part 1 — Department overview (open listening, ~15 min)

**Primary:**
- In your own words, what does your department actually own, start to finish?
- If you had to explain your role to someone brand new, what would you say you personally do versus what you hand off to Rayna, Alejandro, or the VAs?
- What's the part of your job that would be hardest for someone else to pick up if you were out for two weeks?

**Follow-ups:**
- Is there anything in that description that isn't captured anywhere — not in Salesforce, not in Mighty/Justice Bolt, not written down?
- What's changed about your day-to-day since the two VAs came on?

### Part 2 — Workflow specifics (~30 min)

**Underwriting tiering and judgment**
- *Primary:* Walk me through what actually makes a case "Tier 1" versus something that needs your judgment. What are you specifically checking for when you vet what an attorney has told you?
- *Follow-up:* Where's the line, in practice, not in theory — has anything that looked like Tier 1 turned out to need your review anyway?
- *Follow-up:* Is there a dollar or complexity threshold you use, even informally, or is it purely case-by-case feel?

**Intake and the VA layer**
- *Primary:* How's the two-VA setup actually working now that they're a few months in? Where do they hand off to you or to Rayna?
- *Follow-up:* Is that document-collection process feeling like a real system, or still clunky the way it was described early on?

**Rayna, Leifert/Alejandro, and Tier 1 growth**
- *Primary:* Where does Tier 1 training actually stand right now — who's doing it, who's ready, who isn't?
- *Follow-up:* I have two names in my notes — Leifert and Alejandro — for the same kind of role. Are those the same person, or two different people?

**Granular first-touch documentation (Howie's kickoff ask)**
- *Primary:* Howie asked for more detail on how something comes in the door in the first place — website, email, call — and every step Salesforce takes to acknowledge and process it. Can you walk me through that from the very first contact?
- *Follow-up:* Where does that first-touch handling break down most often, if it does?

**Institutional knowledge and your broader DOO role**
- *Primary:* Now that you're the one I'm working through to get direction to the team, how's that actually landing on your plate — more meetings, more relaying, more decisions routed through you?
- *Follow-up:* Is there anything on your plate right now that feels like it shouldn't be yours, but has ended up there anyway?

**Open items worth a direct answer, if time allows**
- Tammy's status — did the 90-day trial happen, and did it stay underwriting-only?
- What system is actually used to send and track agreements once you approve a case? (This is Yasmine's step next, but useful to hear your side of the hand-off.)

## 4. Easy SOP Re-Training (last 15 minutes — same for all three calls)

This is a refresher, not a first introduction — all three attended the July session, but Howie's asked for more guidance and JC committed to closing that gap 1:1. Keep it tight and practical: confirm they know the loop, unblock whatever's actually stopped them, and get commitment to a next dictation before the call ends.

**1. Quick check-in (1–2 min)**

*"Before we wrap — how's the dictation process been going since we covered it? Have you gotten a chance to try it on any of your workflows yet?"*

Listen for: never started, started and got stuck, or did one and isn't sure what's next. This determines which of the next two sub-sections to spend more time on.

**2. Re-cover the method, fast (5–6 min)**

If they haven't started or are shaky on it, re-walk the loop:

- **What "dictating" means:** talking through a workflow out loud, in the amount of detail you'd naturally give a new hire — rough and spoken is fine, nothing needs to be polished.
- **The six things to hit**, as a mental checklist, not a form:
  - What do you need to start? (**Inputs**)
  - Where does that come from? (**Source**)
  - What software/tools do you touch? (**Systems**)
  - Who else is involved? (**Owners**)
  - What comes out the other end? (**Outputs**)
  - Where does that go next? (**Hand-off**)
- **The Claude prompt** (they paste this into their own Claude chat, then just start talking):

> I'm going to describe my role and how I currently handle **[workflow name]** at Capital Financing. Ask me clarifying questions one at a time as I go if anything is unclear. When I say I'm done, write up everything I've described as a clean, numbered, step-by-step SOP that someone with no context could follow. Flag anything ambiguous or unconfirmed as `[TO CONFIRM]` rather than guessing.

- **The loop:** paste prompt → talk through the workflow → Claude asks follow-ups → say "I'm done" → Claude writes the SOP → email the finished text to JC.
- One workflow per sitting is fine. They'll repeat this once per distinct workflow in their role, on their own time.

**3. Unblock whatever's actually stopped them (5–6 min)**

If they've tried it, ask directly:

- *"What made you stop, or what felt unclear when you were doing it?"*
- Common blockers to check for: not sure which workflow to start on first, not sure how much detail is "enough," Claude login/access issue, or just hasn't found the time.
- If it's a login/access issue, confirm they can get into the Capital Financing Claude workspace live on the call rather than leaving it for later.
- If it's "which workflow first," point them to their priority item from Part 2 above (their most institutionally-fragile or highest-open-question workflow), and remind them the goal is to keep going after that one, not stop at one.

**4. Close with a specific commitment**

Before ending the call, get a concrete next step, not a vague "I'll try to get to it":

*"What's one workflow you can dictate before we talk next, and roughly when?"*

Write down whatever they commit to — it's the thing to check on at the next touchpoint.
