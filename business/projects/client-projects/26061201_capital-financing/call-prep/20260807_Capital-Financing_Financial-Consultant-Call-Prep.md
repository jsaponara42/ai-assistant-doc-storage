## Content

### How to use this doc, live

- Same rules as the department-lead prep doc: don't read verbatim, lead with Primary questions, use Follow-ups only where an answer is thin.
- **Don't personalize the opener with performance specifics.** Unlike the department leads, these three have documented performance gaps (follow-up inconsistency, CRM resistance, ratio imbalance) — the point of this call is to hear their actual process in their own words, not to open with a critique dressed up as a question. Keep the opener focused on role and workflow, not their numbers.
- If something performance-related comes up naturally in their own answer, that's fine to follow — just don't lead there.
- The **superpower question** and the **closing question** below are deliberately placed — superpower early, once they're warmed up but still in the open-listening window, and the closing question last, after the SOP segment, as the final thing said before ending the call.

---

## Opening frame (say this first)

*"I want to spend most of this call just hearing how you actually work — what a lead looks like when it lands with you, what you do with it, and where things get followed through versus where they don't. There's no wrong answer here. I'd rather hear the real version than the tidy version. Tell me — walk me through what a typical week looks like for you."*

---

## Part 1 — Role overview (open listening, ~20 min)

**Primary:**
- Walk me through a typical week — where does a lead actually come from, and what do you do with it the moment you get it?
- What's your own definition of a "good" lead versus one you know isn't going anywhere?
- **What's your superpower? What do you do in this role that you think gives you an advantage over other consultants?**

**Follow-ups:**
- Where do most of your leads come from today — Howie directly, conferences, referrals, your own outbound?
- What does "closing" actually look like for you, mechanically — what has to happen for you to consider a lead done?
- How much of what you do lives in Salesforce versus your own notes, memory, or personal system?

---

## Part 2 — Workflow specifics (~25 min)

**Follow-up cadence and discipline**
- *Primary:* Once you've made first contact with a lead, walk me through what happens next — how do you decide when and how to follow up again?
- *Follow-up:* Is there a point where you decide a lead is dead, or does it just fade out? How do you know when to stop?
- *Follow-up:* When you were at a conference and got handed a segmented list afterward, what did you actually do with it once it landed in your inbox?

**Case-expense vs. pre-settlement split**
- *Primary:* When a lead comes in, how do you decide whether it's a pre-settlement conversation or a case-expense conversation — or does that decision happen later?
- *Follow-up:* Is there a type of lead you gravitate toward, and if so, why?

**Onboarding calls (case-expense)**
- *Primary:* Walk me through what you actually say and do on a case-expense onboarding call with a new law firm — what do you make sure they understand before you hang up?
- *Follow-up:* Has a firm ever come back with something submitted wrong or incomplete after onboarding? What happened?

**CRM logging**
- *Primary:* How do you currently log what's happening with a lead — notes, activities, stage changes? Walk me through it as you'd actually do it, not the ideal version.
- *Follow-up:* What makes logging something in Salesforce feel worth the time, versus something you skip?

**Client authority and routing**
- *Primary:* When a client or prospect you're working has a question, do they come to you directly, or do they still sometimes reach out to Howie instead?
- *Follow-up:* If it's the latter — any sense of why? Is there something about how you introduce yourself or set expectations early on that affects that?

**Whale-hunting / prospecting approach**
- *Primary:* How do you currently decide who to spend time chasing — is it more volume-based, or are you already being selective about bigger, higher-value firms?
- *Follow-up:* What research do you actually do on a firm or prospect before reaching out, if any?

## Closing question (ask last, after the SOP segment)

*"Last thing — is there anything you think I should've asked you that I didn't ask?"*

Leave real room for this one. It's often where the most honest or unexpected material comes out, precisely because it wasn't prompted by a specific question.
