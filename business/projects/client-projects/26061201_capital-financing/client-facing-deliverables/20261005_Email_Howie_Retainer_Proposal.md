## Content

**Subject:** Working together going forward: the proposal

Hi Howie,

Thanks for the time today. Here's the write-up I promised, so you can look it over and we can talk once you've met Martina.

**Where we landed**

You're right that the first job is rolling out what we built. That needs project management more than it needs an engineer, so we hold off on hiring one until you know what's on your plate and who owns each piece. Some of the quick wins need no engineer at all, because Kaz, Margarita, and the project manager can take them. The engineer comes in when the builds get complex, and you tell me when it's time.

**The CTO retainer: $2,000 a month, with a fractional project manager**

- I keep the opportunity map moving: delegating and implementing it with the project manager, Kaz, and Margarita.
- I find and manage any vendors who build pieces of it.
- New ideas, from you or from me, get scored against the map so the right things happen in the right order.
- When the engineer comes on, I interview them, write the specs, and review what they build. They report to me, so you don't have to talk to an engineer.
- I keep the technology strategy written down, so if the engineer leaves, the next one starts with full context and nothing is lost.

**Why the price depends on project management**

Project management is its own full-time job. If I do the wrangling, it takes far more of my time and the retainer would be $5,000 to $7,000 a month. That's not a good use of your money or my skills. A fractional project manager should cost well below a fractional CTO, and she's a separate fee.

**What this costs compared with hiring**

A full-time strategist in my role runs $200,000 to $250,000 in salary, close to $300,000 with benefits. A full-time engineer is another $125,000 to $175,000. Two full-time hires is $400,000 to $500,000 a year. The fractional version is $3,000 a month for the engineer and $2,000 a month for me, which is $60,000 a year. Until you hire the engineer, it's just the $2,000.

**Projects, priced separately and only when you're ready**

I'd keep these out of the retainer. Folding them in would raise the monthly price for work we haven't started.

- **Claude power-user coaching, one on one with you.** Five to ten hours together on your real work, until you open Claude and know exactly what you're doing. After that I can train the rest of the team. Price: [PRICE].
- **Sales team skills.** Three skills built for the financial consultants, based on what they do repeatedly, plus training on how to use them: $1,500 for the build and $500 for the training. Once you're comfortable in Claude, you may build some of these yourself.
- **Sharing the engineer across two companies.** If we run the same opportunity map for Injury Specialists, the engineer has more to do and the cost splits between both. We can look at this later.

**Next steps**

1. You meet Martina. After that we settle the retainer.
2. I'll help Audrey get going in Claude when she reaches out.
3. I'll send you a line to add to the IT ticket about Fireflies and external guests.

Let me know what questions come up.

JC
