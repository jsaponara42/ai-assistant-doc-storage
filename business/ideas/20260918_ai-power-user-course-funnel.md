## Summary

The current AI & Automation Roadmap offer is too high-ticket to advertise against directly, which caps volume and starves the sales-call pipeline. Drawing on Alex Hormozi's offer framework, the fix is a cheap, easy-to-advertise entry point — an "AI Power User" course — that funnels upward through a sequence of increasingly expensive offers, ending in a managed-foreign-talent retainer instead of JC's own (expensive) implementation time.

## Content

### The core problem
- The AI & Automation Roadmap offer is priced too high to run as a cold/paid-ads entry point.
- Without an affordable front door, volume stays low, which starves the number of sales calls generated, which stalls pipeline growth overall.
- Needs an offer structure (per Hormozi) with a cheap, compelling entry point that upsells step by step into the existing high-ticket services.

### Proposed funnel (low → high ticket)

1. **"AI Power User" course** — the entry point
   - Low monthly price, group/membership format
   - Curriculum: everything JC currently does personally — Obsidian, markdown files, possibly VS Code, MCP connectors, etc. — taught as a repeatable system
   - Purpose: cheap enough to advertise directly, generates volume, and every member gets a follow-up call (built-in upsell mechanism)

2. **Private "AI Power User" tutoring** — first upsell
   - Sold on the follow-up call every course member receives
   - ~$5,000 for roughly a week / ~10 hours of concentrated one-on-one tutoring
   - Positioned as a fast-track version of the course content, done personally

3. **AI & Automation Roadmap (business version)** — second upsell
   - Targets private-tutoring clients who are business owners with the budget
   - ~$10,000 engagement (the existing product)
   - Open question: whether a "small business" variant of the earlier steps is needed to reach owners who wouldn't otherwise qualify — shelved for now, not decided

4. **Leave-behind offer: managed foreign talent** — final upsell, ongoing revenue
   - Rather than JC personally implementing the roadmap (his time is too expensive to sell at scale), recommend hiring foreign talent through Blue Tusk
   - Blue Tusk manages the talent on the client's behalf; client still only communicates with Blue Tusk
   - Because the Roadmap was already built in-house, Blue Tusk already knows how to direct the foreign hire's work
   - Estimated pricing: ~$3,000–$5,000/month depending on management fees added on top

### Why this sequencing makes sense
- Each step is sold to people who already said yes to the previous one — no cold selling required after the initial course-level ad spend.
- The expensive, scarce resource (JC's own implementation time) is removed from the funnel entirely — the managed-talent retainer replaces it with a scalable, lower-margin-but-repeatable revenue stream.
- The Roadmap product doesn't need to change; it just moves further down the funnel instead of being the front door.

### Open questions / acknowledged weak points
- JC flagged that even if this exact sequence isn't the right one, the underlying need — a genuinely great, cheap, high-volume entry offer — is the real problem to solve.
- Unclear whether a full course curriculum (course production is a real lift) is the right first move vs. a lighter entry offer.
- Small-business variant of the funnel not yet scoped.
- Foreign-talent sourcing/management logistics (who, how vetted, what tools, what the management fee actually covers) not yet defined.

## Entry-offer exploration (2026-09-18, post-initial-draft)

The original AI Power User course concept above was pressure-tested in a follow-up conversation and superseded. The course/cohort format itself is being held in reserve, not discarded — see below. Logging the full reasoning trail so it isn't lost.

### Why the course concept didn't survive scrutiny
- Wrong avatar. Become an AI Power User attracts individuals who want a personal skill, not business owners/ops leaders who want their company automated. The Roadmap's actual buyer doesn't want to learn AI, they want it installed. A course funnel risks generating volume that doesn't convert into Roadmap-qualified leads.
- Immediacy problem, restated. The Roadmap is hard to advertise because it reads as pay first, wait weeks, get a document. A cheap entry offer needs to hand over something felt and immediate, not just be cheap.

### Rejected: opportunity snapshot / mini-audit
- Initial fix-attempt: a paid, fast-turnaround Automation Opportunity Snapshot (reusing the pi-firm-snapshot / audit-report-notion skill pipeline), positioned as a scaled-down version of the Capital Financing opportunity map.
- Rejected because: people don't want to be told their opportunities, they already assume AI has huge upside for them. An audit delivers knowledge, not progress. Nobody pays for a first purchase that just tells them something true; they want to feel ahead, not informed. Audits also reintroduce the wait-to-find-out-then-wait-again-to-act problem the entry offer is supposed to solve.

### Rejected: quick-win menu (fixed-recipe automations, installed fast)
- Initial fix-attempt: templatize automations already built more than once for real clients (referral-gap follow-up, Slack digest, CRM to email sync, see the [[20260910_capital-financing-opportunity-map-final]] opportunity map) into a fixed-price, fast-install menu, so buyers get a working change in their business within days.
- Rejected because: this underestimates how long anything takes inside a real business at scale, integration with a client's live systems (testing against their actual CRM/data, handling their edge cases) carries a real time cost regardless of how known the recipe is. This ruled out any offer that requires touching or integrating with a client's live systems as a viable low-price, immediate entry point, that constraint now applies to all options going forward.

### Rejected (for now): free self-serve tool / calculator + ops-leader lead magnet
- Initial fix-attempt: a quantified, instant self-serve tool (a la 20260814_file-chaos-cost-calculator), e.g. an AI-readiness score or a you're-losing-$X/month calculator, positioned as either a free lead magnet or a cheap paid upsell, targeted at ops leaders specifically (a different buyer than the business owner) as a possible lower-cost distribution channel.
- Rejected as a paid offer because: calculators/tools are expected to be free in the current AI content environment, nobody pays $197 to $399 for something that reads as generic content. Still potentially useful as a genuinely free lead magnet / distribution mechanism, just not as the monetized entry point. Not fully discarded, worth revisiting specifically as a free top-of-funnel piece, separate from the paid-entry-offer question.

### Explored, kept on file: paid cohort / operator's group
- Concept: a paid ($197 to $399) cohort or community, reframed away from learn-AI-personally toward Ops Leaders Automating [vertical], live calls, hot-seat problem-solving on real business issues, peer network, structure and accountability. The stated rationale: at this price point, people are paying for access, accountability, and belonging, not information (which is expected free), and belonging delivers the instant feeling of being ahead that content alone can't. Natural upsell path: hot-seat sessions surface this-needs-more-than-we-can-do-here moments that lead straight into private tutoring or the Roadmap.
- Why it's on hold, not built: for JC's target buyer (older, more skeptical, business-owner/ops-leader audience), a paid cohort risks reading as a generic paid-membership-club, a format with a poor reputation (flaky engagement, high expectations, low completion) that could damage credibility with exactly the audience Blue Tusk needs to look high-status to.
- Status: kept as a fallback/reference option, not pursued as the primary direction while other formats are explored.

### Current leading candidate: personalized async teardown
- Concept: productize the existing pi-firm-snapshot-style capability directly. Prospect submits their website/LinkedIn/a few intake questions; within roughly 48 hours they receive a short, personally recorded (Loom-style) video where JC reacts to their specific business, 2 to 3 concrete, specific observations, ending in a clear next-step offer.
- Why it clears the accumulated objections:
  - Immediate (48hr turnaround, no live call required)
  - Feels bespoke/personal (it's genuinely about their business) without being an ongoing relationship or club/membership
  - Async, scales past JC's calendar, unlike a live call or cohort
  - No integration with the client's live systems, self-contained, no implementation tail
  - Natural upsell: ends by pointing at the full Roadmap
- Reuses existing capability: built on the same underlying pipeline as the pi-firm-snapshot skill already in the vault (business/SKILLS/pi-firm-snapshot/SKILL.md).
- Not yet resolved: the earlier objection that audits/snapshots deliver knowledge, not progress, applies here too, softened by (a) it being personal/reactive rather than a generic opportunity list, and (b) the price point and delivery format signaling status/attention rather than go-figure-out-your-opportunities-yourself. Worth stress-testing directly before building.

### Other options generated, not yet pursued
1. Build-to-order single asset, client picks one item off a fixed menu (an outreach sequence, an intake questionnaire, a configured Claude Project/prompt system), fills out a brief, receives a finished self-contained file within days. Fixed scope and price; distinct from the rejected quick-win menu because the deliverable is handed over rather than installed into the client's own systems.
2. Single intensive session, sold as an event not a program, a one-time, capped-seat, paid live working session (solo or small group) with a hard start/end, avoiding the recurring-membership connotations of a cohort while still delivering live-access value.
3. Physical / highly-produced deliverable, a premium, well-designed physical or high-production digital artifact (printed or beautifully designed vertical-specific strategy one-pager, possibly mailed), signals value through production quality rather than content novelty, since content itself is treated as commoditized/free.

### Open tension to resolve before building anything
Generic/scalable entry offers (calculators, generic playbooks) avoid the time cost of touching a client's business, but risk feeling low-value exactly because they're generic, the thing that made the actual Roadmap deliverables compelling was real specificity from real interviews. Whatever gets built needs an explicit answer for where it sits on that specificity-vs-scalability tradeoff.

### Rejected: novelty/spectacle-driven ideas ("Roast My Business", "Beat the Bot", "Clone Yourself", "AI War Room" as entertainment, "60-Second Build")
- Initial fix-attempt: a batch of ideas optimized purely for an immediate emotional yes, comedy, competition, spectacle, watching something get built live, all fully automated/software-only so they'd scale infinitely with zero marginal cost.
- Rejected because: the buyer is a smart, successful executive, not a consumer. Flashy/gimmicky delivery reads as a trick, not a service, and damages credibility for every future paid engagement, since JC's reputation is on the line with each one and the eventual upsell depends on trust built here. Executives don't have time to experiment or noodle; they want the power of AI and automation applied to their business, they just don't know where to start and their attempts to delegate that discovery fail. The offer has to be visibly worth more than something free, not just entertaining.
- Kept, reframed: the underlying idea of a structured, rigorous "war room" (multiple AI perspectives stress-testing a decision, using JC's real cross-client framework rather than generic brainstorming) is still plausible as a serious decision-support product, not as entertainment. Not pursued further once the async teardown was reconfirmed as the leading direction, but the reframing (deliver a forced, defensible decision, not a list or a show) is a useful principle for any future version of this.

## Async teardown: refined offer mechanic (2026-09-18)

Following confirmation that the personalized async teardown (above) is still the leading candidate, the intake process and pricing/qualification mechanic were worked out in more detail.

### Fast, high-signal intake design
- Pull anything public (website, LinkedIn) automatically first, reusing the pi-firm-snapshot pipeline, never ask a question Google could already answer.
- Concrete mechanic decided on: a Google Doc template with a fixed set of questions, prospect makes their own copy and uses voice dictation to answer directly into each question, rather than typing. Keeps the "talk, don't type" benefit (faster, richer answers from busy executives) without needing custom voice-intake tooling.
- Prospect is encouraged to send the doc around to their own team so specific people can answer the questions they personally know best, this both improves answer quality/specificity (echoing the earlier "specificity vs. scalability" tension, this is a way to get real specificity without costing JC any time) and mirrors the same team-distribution instinct that has worked in real engagements (e.g. multiple stakeholders interviewed for the Capital Financing opportunity map).

### Pricing and qualification mechanic
- Structured as an introductory offer: a $500 value, positioned as "free" if the prospect completes certain conditions (their "homework").
- Homework under consideration: completing the intake doc properly, and possibly referring 3 other qualifying businesses, not finalized. JC is open to adding more homework/conditions beyond referrals if useful.
- Qualification gate: prospects must qualify for the free offer, not open to everyone who wants a free teardown. Qualification requires collecting real information upfront: business size, and willingness/budget to eventually purchase the AI & Automation Roadmap. This serves two purposes at once, it keeps the free version from being a cost center on unqualified leads, and it front-loads exactly the information needed to know whether the Roadmap upsell is realistic before JC invests any time.
- This was JC's own resolution of the three refund-mechanic options discussed (A: refund on completion, B: refund as Roadmap credit, C: refund if JC doesn't deliver value), the actual direction taken is closer to option A in structure (earn it back by completing conditions) but reframed as "free upfront if qualified + homework done" rather than "pay then get refunded," combined with a hard qualification gate that wasn't part of the original three options. Refund/payment mechanics not fully finalized, this section supersedes the earlier three-option framing as the working direction.

### Still open
- Exact homework requirements (just the intake doc, or also referrals, and if referrals, how many and how verified)
- Exact qualification questions/thresholds (business size cutoff, budget/willingness signal to ask for without being off-putting this early)
- Whether the $500 "value" is ever actually charged upfront and refunded, or simply waived entirely for qualified prospects who complete homework, distinct mechanics with different cash-flow and commitment-device implications
- What happens for a qualified prospect who doesn't complete the homework, still get the teardown at a paid price, or excluded entirely
