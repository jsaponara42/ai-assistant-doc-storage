## Content

### The core problem
- The AI & Automation Roadmap offer is priced too high to run as a cold/paid-ads entry point.
- Without an affordable front door, volume stays low, which starves the number of sales calls generated, which stalls pipeline growth overall.
- Needs an offer structure (per Hormozi) with a cheap, compelling entry point that upsells step by step into the existing high-ticket services.

### Proposed funnel (low → high ticket)

1. **"AI Power User" course** — the entry point
   - Low monthly price, group/membership format
   - Curriculum: everything JC currently does personally — Obsidian, markdown files, possibly VS Code, MCP connectors, etc. — taught as a repeatable system
   - Purpose: cheap enough to advertise directly, generates volume, and every member gets a follow-up call (built-in upsell mechanism)

2. **Private "AI Power User" tutoring** — first upsell
   - Sold on the follow-up call every course member receives
   - ~$5,000 for roughly a week / ~10 hours of concentrated one-on-one tutoring
   - Positioned as a fast-track version of the course content, done personally

3. **AI & Automation Roadmap (business version)** — second upsell
   - Targets private-tutoring clients who are business owners with the budget
   - ~$10,000 engagement (the existing product)
   - Open question: whether a "small business" variant of the earlier steps is needed to reach owners who wouldn't otherwise qualify — shelved for now, not decided

4. **Leave-behind offer: managed foreign talent** — final upsell, ongoing revenue
   - Rather than JC personally implementing the roadmap (his time is too expensive to sell at scale), recommend hiring foreign talent through Blue Tusk
   - Blue Tusk manages the talent on the client's behalf; client still only communicates with Blue Tusk
   - Because the Roadmap was already built in-house, Blue Tusk already knows how to direct the foreign hire's work
   - Estimated pricing: ~$3,000–$5,000/month depending on management fees added on top

### Why this sequencing makes sense
- Each step is sold to people who already said yes to the previous one — no cold selling required after the initial course-level ad spend.
- The expensive, scarce resource (JC's own implementation time) is removed from the funnel entirely — the managed-talent retainer replaces it with a scalable, lower-margin-but-repeatable revenue stream.
- The Roadmap product doesn't need to change; it just moves further down the funnel instead of being the front door.

### Open questions / acknowledged weak points
- JC flagged that even if this exact sequence isn't the right one, the underlying need — a genuinely great, cheap, high-volume entry offer — is the real problem to solve.
- Unclear whether a full course curriculum (course production is a real lift) is the right first move vs. a lighter entry offer.
- Small-business variant of the funnel not yet scoped.
- Foreign-talent sourcing/management logistics (who, how vetted, what tools, what the management fee actually covers) not yet defined.
