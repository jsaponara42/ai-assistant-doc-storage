# SOP — Discovery-Phase Call Prep by Role

## Summary

This SOP governs how to prepare questionnaires for 1:1 discovery-phase calls with people inside a client organization — executives/owners, department leads, and individual contributors such as sales/BDR staff. It generalizes the pattern first used on the Capital Financing engagement (see [[business/projects/client-projects/26061201_capital-financing/call-prep/20260807_Capital-Financing_Department-1-on-1-Call-Prep_Christy-Danielle-Yasmine]], [[business/projects/client-projects/26061201_capital-financing/call-prep/20260807_Capital-Financing_Financial-Consultant-Call-Prep]], and [[business/projects/client-projects/26061201_capital-financing/call-prep/20260807_Capital-Financing_Howie-Call-Prep_Time-Value-and-Wishlist]]) into a reusable, company-agnostic method. The core idea: different roles need structurally different call preps — an executive call is about time, leverage, and priorities; an operations/department-lead call is about ownership, judgment, and process detail; a sales/individual-contributor call is about day-to-day workflow and what actually drives their results. Using the wrong shape on the wrong role wastes the call.

## Previous Methods (Superseded)

This is the first documented version of this process. It formalizes an ad hoc pattern developed live on the Capital Financing engagement rather than replacing a prior documented SOP.

## Current Process

Building a call-prep document takes four stages: gather what's already known, choose the role archetype(s) needed, write the document using the matching template below, and set the time allocation per section. A single call-prep document may contain multiple role sections if several people share a role (e.g., one shared questionnaire for three sales reps) — see Stage 3.

### Stage 1 — Gather What's Already Known

1. Before writing a single question, pull everything already documented about the company and the specific person: the discovery brief, any workflow map, any running "wants" or wishlist document, and prior call notes or learnings files for this client. The goal of every opener in Stage 3 is to demonstrate real preparation, so skipping this step produces generic questions that read as if no homework was done.
2. Note anything already known about the person's working style, communication preferences, or sensitivities (e.g., a Persuader/intuitive decision-maker who acts fast without a framework in front of them, or someone touchy about a specific prior conflict). This becomes the "How to run this call" guidance in Stage 3, not a question to ask them directly.
3. Note any sensitive or adjacent topics that belong to a different conversation entirely (an unrelated management conflict, a personnel/performance issue, a pricing negotiation). Flag these explicitly as out of scope for this call so the person running it doesn't accidentally blur two conversations together.

### Stage 2 — Choose the Role Archetype(s)

4. Identify which of the three archetypes below applies to each person on the call list. Most client organizations map cleanly onto these three; if someone doesn't fit any of them, use the Executive/Owner template as the closest fallback and adjust the domain-specific questions in Part 2 to their actual function.

   - **Executive/Owner** — anyone whose primary lens is the whole business: founders, CEOs, owners, partners. The call is about time, leverage, and priorities, not their own task-level workflow.
   - **Operations/Department Lead** — anyone who owns a function or team with defined process responsibility: ops directors, controllers, department managers, senior individual contributors with process ownership. The call is about what they own, how they exercise judgment, and workflow specifics within their department.
   - **Sales/Individual Contributor** — anyone doing repeatable, individually-executed work without broader process ownership: sales reps, BDRs, customer-facing consultants, support staff. The call is about their actual day-to-day workflow and what makes them effective, not department-level ownership.

5. If multiple people share the same role (e.g., three sales reps), use one shared question set for all of them rather than writing a separate document per person — see the Sales/Individual Contributor template's note on this. If people in the same role archetype have meaningfully different tenure, performance profile, or history (a new hire vs. a ten-year veteran), keep the shared question set but do not personalize the opener with performance specifics — see Stage 3, step 8.

### Stage 3 — Write the Document Using the Matching Template

6. Create one document per engagement round, not per person, unless the roles genuinely require separate documents (e.g., one executive is meaningfully different from every department lead). Use section headers to separate each person or role group within the single document.

7. For every section, write an **opening frame** — a short, spoken statement (not a question) that states something already believed to be true about their role or department, based on Stage 1 research, then explicitly invites correction. This does two things: it proves preparation, and it turns any correction into new data rather than embarrassment. Never write an opener as a list of facts — write it as something that could actually be said out loud in one breath.

   Example shape: *"From what I understand, you own [X], and I know [specific known detail]. Tell me how you'd actually describe it, and I'll fill in from there."*

8. For Operations/Department Lead and Sales/Individual Contributor sections, do not lead the opener or Part 1 questions with known performance gaps, conflicts, or criticisms, even if those are documented. The point of the call is to hear their honest account of their own process — opening with a critique dressed as a question collapses that. If a known issue is relevant, let it surface naturally in their own answer and follow it there instead of leading with it.

9. Build each role section using the matching template:

**EXECUTIVE/OWNER TEMPLATE**

```
## [Name] — [Title]

### How to run this call
[Working-style notes from Stage 1: decision-making style, pace,
sensitivities, anything to explicitly avoid raising on this call
and why.]

### Opening frame (say this first)
[One spoken statement per Stage 3, step 7.]

### Part 1 — Time & Value (~20 min)
Primary:
- If you could hand off everything except the two or three things
  only you can do, what would those be?
- Walk me through last week — where did your time actually go,
  versus where you wish it had gone?
Follow-ups:
- [2-4 follow-ups probing what's habit vs. necessity]

### Part 2 — [Domain-Specific Process Detail] (~25-30 min)
One subsection per known want, initiative, or open decision this
person owns. Each subsection: a Primary question that asks them to
walk through the actual current process, plus 1-2 Follow-ups that
probe for what's vague, undecided, or contradicted elsewhere.

### Closing questions (ask last)
- If we only fixed one thing from today, what would move the
  needle most for you personally?
- Is there anything you think I should've asked you that I didn't?
```

**OPERATIONS/DEPARTMENT LEAD TEMPLATE**

```
## [Name] — [Title]

### Opening frame (say this first)
[One spoken statement per Stage 3, step 7.]

### Part 1 — Department overview (open listening, ~15 min)
Primary:
- In your own words, what does your department actually own,
  start to finish?
- What's the part of your job that would be hardest for someone
  else to pick up if you were out for two weeks?
Follow-ups:
- Is there anything in that description that isn't captured
  anywhere — not in any system, not written down?

### Part 2 — Workflow specifics (~30 min)
One subsection per known workflow, judgment call, or open question
this person's department owns. Each subsection: a Primary question
asking them to walk through the actual process, plus Follow-ups
targeting known gaps, unreconciled facts, or `[TO CONFIRM]` items
from existing documentation.

### Part 3 — [Optional: process/method re-training segment]
Include only if this person needs re-training on a shared method
(documentation practice, a tool, a reporting habit). Omit entirely
if not applicable — do not pad this section in for its own sake.
```

**SALES/INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTOR TEMPLATE**

```
## [Role] — shared questionnaire for [names]

### Opening frame (say this first)
[One spoken statement, role-level not person-level — do not
personalize with individual performance data. Focus on wanting
the real, unpolished version of how they work.]

### Part 1 — Role overview (open listening, ~20 min)
Primary:
- Walk me through a typical week — where does work actually come
  from, and what do you do with it the moment you get it?
- What's your superpower? What do you do in this role that you
  think gives you an advantage over your peers?
Follow-ups:
- [2-3 follow-ups on sourcing, definition of "done," and how much
  lives in a system vs. their own head]

### Part 2 — Workflow specifics (~25 min)
One subsection per known step in their actual workflow (first
contact, follow-up cadence, logging/documentation habits, handoffs
to other roles). Each subsection: a Primary question asking them
to walk through what they actually do, not the ideal version, plus
Follow-ups on judgment calls and edge cases.

### Part 3 — [Optional: process/method re-training segment]
Same rule as the Operations/Department Lead template — include
only if applicable, reference a shared script if one already
exists elsewhere rather than re-writing it per document.

### Closing question (ask last)
- Is there anything you think I should've asked you that I didn't?
```

10. Adjust the time allocations in each template to fit the actual scheduled call length — the ratios shown (roughly 60-70% overview/value, remainder specifics, closing questions last) are a starting point, not a fixed rule. A 30-minute call compresses proportionally; a 90-minute call gets more room in Part 2.
11. If a re-training or teaching segment (Part 3 in the Operations and Sales templates) is shared across multiple people or documents, write it once in whichever document is easiest to reference later, and point other documents to it with a wikilink rather than duplicating it.

### Stage 4 — Final Read-Through

12. Read the finished document once as if you were the person running the call, not writing it. Confirm every opening frame could actually be said out loud in one breath, every Primary question could stand alone without the Follow-ups, and nothing in Part 1 accidentally leads with a known criticism (Stage 3, step 8).
13. Save the document per Stage naming and location conventions: inside the specific client project's `call-prep/` folder, not in this SOPs folder. This SOP defines the method; the output belongs with the client engagement.

## What to Track

After each round of calls run from a document built with this SOP, note the following in the client's project-learnings file:

- Which opening-frame corrections surfaced new facts, and what those facts were.
- Whether the 45/15 (or equivalent) time split actually held, and if not, which direction it drifted.
- Any question that fell flat or produced a one-word answer — worth rewriting before the next round.
- Anything said in response to the closing "anything I should've asked" question — this is often the highest-signal answer in the call and should never be treated as a throwaway.
- Any promise or commitment made live on the call (by either side) — these need the same tracking discipline as promises made in kickoff calls.

## What Is Not In Scope

- This SOP does not cover the underlying discovery brief or workflow-map documents that Stage 1 draws from — those are separate deliverables with their own structure.
- This SOP does not cover the Easy SOP dictation method itself (the technique for turning someone's spoken workflow description into a written SOP) — that is a separate, already-documented method referenced by the optional Part 3 segment, not defined here.
- This SOP does not cover how to run the call itself beyond the question bank and time allocation — tone, rapport-building, and live facilitation judgment are not written procedures.
- This SOP does not cover role archetypes outside the three defined here (e.g., board members, external vendors/partners, HR). Use the Executive/Owner template as the closest fallback per Stage 2, step 4, until enough of these calls accumulate to justify a fourth template.

## Next Review

Review this SOP after it has been used to build call-prep documents for at least three different client engagements. At that point, check whether the three role archetypes have held up as sufficient, whether a fourth archetype (e.g., a dedicated "vendor/partner" template) has become clearly necessary, and whether the opening-frame and closing-question patterns are consistently producing useful corrections and new information across different companies and industries.
