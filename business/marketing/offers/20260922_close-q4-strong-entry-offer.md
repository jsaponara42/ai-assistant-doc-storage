## Summary
Reworking the async teardown entry offer under a new "Close Q4 Strong" positioning, aimed at non-technical, non-relationship-marketed business owners, using Alex Hormozi's $100M Offers / $100M Leads frameworks as the structuring lens.

## Context
Pivoted away from the PI-firm-specific framing in [[20260920_async-teardown-entry-offer]]. This note captures the general-business version of the entry offer, worked through Hormozi's "Seven Steps to an Effective Lead Magnet" (from $100M Leads) starting at Step 1–2, plus the underlying principle that lead magnets should get Grand Slam Offer treatment — insanely good, not just "good enough because it's free."

## Content

### Reference: Seven Steps to an Effective Lead Magnet (Hormozi, $100M Leads)
1. Figure out the problem you want to solve and who to solve it for
2. Figure out how to solve it
3. Figure out how to deliver it
4. Test what to name it
5. Make it easy to consume
6. Make it darn good
7. Make it easy for them to tell you they want more

Underlying principle: free offers deserve the same "insanely good" treatment as paid offers — the business that provides the most value wins, period.

### Step 1 — Problem + Who (current draft)
- **Who:** Owners of established, successful businesses with no in-house engineering/technical talent — never needed a technical co-founder or dev team to compete. Explicitly excluding industries where relationship-based or conference-based marketing is the primary buying channel (e.g. law, construction, commercial real estate, insurance brokerage, wealth management, medical/dental, manufacturing with rep networks, staffing/recruiting) — these buyers don't respond to cold digital offers the way this funnel needs.
- **Likely good-fit industries (cold-digital-responsive buyers):** multi-location retail, restaurants/hospitality groups, e-commerce/physical product brands, accounting & bookkeeping firms, property management, logistics/trucking/distribution, cleaning/facilities services, education & training companies, fitness/wellness studio groups, auto dealerships/service groups.
- **Problem:** AI has created a sense that the ground shifted under a business model that used to be safe without technical talent. Not information-overwhelm anymore — more like **status anxiety + FOMO with no clear translation to their specific business.** They don't know what "competing on AI" even means for a business like theirs.
- **Positioning hook (working):** "Close Q4 Strong" — gives urgency without requiring AI-specific language in the hook. E.g. "See exactly where AI can close gaps in your business before year-end — no tech team required."

### Step 2 — How to solve it (revised — funnel now has 3 stages)

Initial Step 2 draft (single paid/waived teardown as the only "how to solve it") has been superseded by a three-stage funnel, splitting automatable value (free) from human-touch value (priced):

**Stage 1 — Free automated magnet (cold traffic only):**
Built as ONE underlying automated report engine, marketed as two different hooks/landing pages to A/B test which emotional driver converts better:
- **Competitive Reality Check** — "here's what businesses like yours are actually doing with AI right now, and what's just hype." Pulls 3–5 comparable-business examples, ranks proven vs. speculative, shows where they stand relative to that picture. Leads with curiosity/FOMO.
- **Risk Exposure Map** — identifies which parts of their business are exposed if competitors adopt AI first, shows what "losing ground" would concretely look like, pairs each risk with the smallest first move. Leads with fear/urgency.
- Same engine can deliver both analyses in the actual report regardless of which hook brought them in — only the marketing/positioning differs, not necessarily the build.
- Fully automated, no human time cost → no qualification gate needed, open to any cold opt-in.

**Stage 2 — Priced/waived Opportunity Teardown (real person, JC's eyes):**
This is the mechanic already designed in [[20260920_async-teardown-entry-offer]] — 2–3 specific, dollarized opportunities, personally reviewed by JC, $500 value waived for qualified + homework-complete prospects. Because a real person's judgment is involved, this is the one that can legitimately carry a price tag (unlike Stage 1).

**Stage 3 — AI & Automation Roadmap ($10K core offer):**
Existing core offer, upsold at the end of the Teardown.

**Routing logic by traffic temperature:**
- **Cold traffic** (ads, content, cold outreach) → Stage 1 (free magnet) → qualifies into Stage 2 (Teardown)
- **Warm traffic** (referrals, past contacts, engaged leads) → skips Stage 1 entirely, goes straight to Stage 2 — the qualification gate does the filtering instead of the free magnet, since trust is already partially built

This mirrors the warm vs. cold split already reflected in existing SOPs (`SOP-Cold-Outreach-PI-Firms`, `SOP-Warm-and-Cold-Reconnection-Outreach`) and Hormozi's own $100M Leads logic — warm audiences need less friction before the ask, cold audiences need to be warmed up first.

### Emotional sequencing (marketing, to refine later)
Rather than picking one emotion, sequence three across the funnel:
1. **Fear/FOMO** opens the door (ad/hook) — "AI is changing what 'competitive' means, and you didn't need to know how to code to compete before."
2. **Opportunity** drives the opt-in (offer promise) — "The businesses moving now, without an engineering team, are the ones setting themselves up for Q1."
3. **Relief** is the experience of the teardown itself — specific, prioritized, jargon-free — which builds trust and sets up the Roadmap upsell.
