## Summary
Five attraction-offer concepts (adapted from Alex Hormozi's $100M Money Models framework) for driving booked sales calls on the AI & Automation Roadmap — now the flagship intro product upselling into a retainer, targeting Directors of Operations / COOs at companies under $50M/year revenue.

## Context
Follows a lead-magnet brainstorm session. Key decisions locked in before this:
- The **AI & Automation Roadmap** is the flagship intro offer; lead magnets should make booking a Roadmap sales call the obvious next step.
- ICP shifted from PI law firms specifically to **Directors of Operations / COOs at sub-$50M revenue companies**, any industry — this makes cold LinkedIn outreach and content-led distribution far more viable than they were for the PI-attorney ICP (who barely use LinkedIn).
- Referral/introducer channel remains strongest — Martina Madrid Sebring (Maycomb's fractional COO, who has her own separate client roster) flagged as a high-value introducer candidate, not just a client.
- Prior research: [[business/research/2026-04-30-zero-response-pipeline-diagnosis]] found cold-at-volume failed for PI attorneys (deliverability + trust problem) — informs caution on cold-email-at-volume even for the new ICP until fundamentals are fixed.

## Content

### 1. Giveaway — "Free Roadmap" Raffle
Grand prize: one full Roadmap done free (state retail value, e.g. "$6,000 value"). Entry requires real qualifying info (revenue band, team size, biggest ops bottleneck) — doubles as market research. Everyone who doesn't win gets a steep, time-limited discount ("Automation Scholarship," 50% off, 7-day expiry). 5–7 day entry window with daily value-add updates. Solves the cold-outreach trust problem via curiosity-driven opt-in instead of a cold pitch.

### 2. Decoy Offer — Snapshot vs. Roadmap, Side by Side
The free/cheap Ops Waste Snapshot or diagnostic quiz *is* the decoy — intentionally limited (self-serve, no expert review, public-signal-only) — presented right next to the Roadmap (full workflow mapping, live sessions, sequenced plan, SOPs delivered). Ask "are you here for the quick free look, or the actual fix?" to route straight to the Roadmap pitch when appropriate. Unifies the earlier multi-lead-magnet debate into one funnel.

### 3. Win Your Money Back — Roadmap Risk Reversal
Mid-funnel closer, used live on the sales call: Roadmap fee refunded (or converted to retainer credit) if a defined number of concrete automation opportunities isn't surfaced, or action-gated on completing discovery sessions. Removes the "what if this doesn't find anything useful" objection for a still-unproven firm. Natural upsell moment: credit toward retainer if they hit the goal.

### 4. Pay Less Now or Pay More Later — Booking Friction Killer
Pay later: $0 due to book, invoice on delivery (satisfaction-gated). Pay now: 20–30% off plus a bonus (e.g., free SOP documentation add-on). Removes upfront trust risk for an unfamiliar firm. Best paired with the risk-reversal guarantee above.

### 5. Buy X Get Y Free — Retainer Bundle (later-stage)
Roadmap + first quick-win implementation free (formalize the existing discretionary Maycomb SOW practice as a standing offer). Retainer bundling (e.g., 6 months signed, get 1–2 free). Post-Roadmap upsell conversation, not a top-of-funnel hook — lower priority right now.

### Recommended starting combo
**Giveaway** (top-of-funnel hook, solves the "unknown firm, cold audience" problem) feeding into **Pay Less Now/Pay Later + Win Your Money Back** as the live sales-call close (removes financial risk of booking with an unfamiliar firm). Decoy framing kept as the answer to "what's the difference between the free thing and the paid thing" rather than a separate campaign.
