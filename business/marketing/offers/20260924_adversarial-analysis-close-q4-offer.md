## Summary
Ran the full funnel through the adversarial-analysis skill in three rounds: positioning, the Stage 1 magnets and intake form, the Stage 2 Teardown, and the Stage 3 Roadmap handoff. The design is mostly sound, but five problems are serious enough to fix before launch:
- The "Q4" hook can't be delivered on.
- The one-day delay is built to fake a human review.
- The $1,500 anchor is never actually charged.
- The $497 Teardown reads as "pay for a sales call."
- The "AI" in every headline contradicts the positioning note.

None of the recommended changes below have been applied to the source notes yet.

JC realized none of the offers or lead-magnet builds had been through [[adversarial-analysis]] before building began. ## Content

### Step 1 — The claim, as a prospect hears it today
"In 5 minutes, a firm I've never heard of will tell me what not using AI is costing my business, so I can close Q4 strong. Then for $497 (normally $1,500), they'll show me 2–3 more opportunities. Then I'll be asked to spend $10K on a plan."

---

### Final revised asset

#### 1. Positioning: retime the hook, remove "AI" from the headline
- **Replace "Close Q4 Strong" as the core promise with "Plan 2027 with real numbers."** Nothing a prospect buys in November gets built before December 31. Year-end is when owners set next year's priorities, and a diagnosis fits planning season honestly. "Q4" can survive as a seasonal subhead in October and November.
- **Urgency comes from the cost, not the calendar.** Every report shows a per-month figure ("every month this stays manual costs about $X"). That is the "gets worse the longer you wait" element the Reveal-Their-Problem magnet needs, which the current mechanic lacks.
- **Lead with their process, sell AI as the fix.** The offer note already says the hook shouldn't need AI language, yet all four headline drafts say "AI."

#### 2. Revised Stage 1 headlines and subheads

**Risk Exposure Map**
- H1: "See What Your Most Manual Process Costs You Each Year, in Hours and Dollars"
- H2: "What Your Busiest Week Is Really Costing You"
- Sub: "5 minutes of questions. A personalized cost breakdown, reviewed by a real person, plus the smallest first step to fix it. No tech team required."

**Competitive Reality Check**
- H1: "What Your Competitors Let Customers Do That You Don't"
- H2: "What Businesses Like Yours Are Actually Automating (and What's Just Hype)"
- Sub: "We look at your local competitors' websites and show you what's worth copying and what isn't. No tech team required."

"Reviewed by a real person" is only allowed if section 3 is adopted.

#### 3. Stage 1 engine and delivery changes
- **Make the one-day hold honest: JC reviews every report before it's sent** (about 5 minutes each at launch volume). The current design holds the report "to create the impression a real person reviewed it." If a prospect ever works that out, and the Teardown call is exactly where they would, trust breaks at the moment you're asking for money. A real review also catches AI errors in dollar figures and site observations before a stranger sees them, and it flags hot leads. Revisit when volume passes roughly 15 reports a week.
- **Show the math on the page:** "You told us: 5–20 a day × 5–15 minutes, done by office staff." The number is theirs, not ours. That defuses "you just multiplied my own answers."
- **Separate total cost from recoverable cost.** Report the current annual cost, then a conservative recoverable share by process type. Claiming AI recovers 100% of the time is the fastest way to lose a skeptical owner.
- **Tame the band math (input to the next build task).** Screen 7 bands can produce a 12× spread (for example, 25 to 300 minutes a day). Use conservative band midpoints and show one "conservative estimate" with the math, not a raw min–max range.
- **Competitive Reality Check guardrails:**
  - Every competitor claim links to the public page it came from.
  - Don't call online booking, chat or instant quotes "AI." Call them what they are: things customers can do without calling.
  - Generic industry examples must be real and citable, or labeled as patterns.

#### 4. Intake form edits (small changes to the Jotform build)
- **Screen 10 no longer acts as an ICP filter.** Anyone filling in the form has already responded to a cold digital offer, so they've self-selected in. Many good-fit industries, like bookkeeping, property management and cleaning, get customers mostly by referral. Keep the question for exposure analysis only.
- **Voice prompts become "Talk or type."** Short typed answers must feel fine; many owners won't speak aloud in a shared office.
- **Add one privacy line near Screen 2:** what's stored, who sees it, and that nothing is shared.
- **Verify "About 5 minutes" in the phone test.** If it runs longer, change the number, not the truth.

#### 5. Stage 2 Teardown: a clear value ladder

| Stage | Promise | Delivers | Held back | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Free report | What one process costs you | 1–2 processes costed (hours, dollars, per month), math shown, smallest first step | Everything beyond one process | Free |
| 2. Teardown | What to do about your top opportunities | 2–3 opportunities with team input, Effort/Impact tiers, **a concrete "how to start" for each** (approach, rough effort, what they'd need), coverage map | Sequencing, ownership, and the other business units | $497 cold / free warm |
| 3. Roadmap | A plan for the whole business | Full discovery across all six units, sequenced plan with owners and timelines | Implementation (see Known weaknesses) | $10K |

The added "how to start" per opportunity is what makes the Teardown progress rather than knowledge. Without it, the Teardown is "the free report, plus one more finding."

#### 6. Revised Stage 2 CTA (cold path)

> **Your report found about $[X] a year going into [process].** For $497, I'll dig into your top 2–3 opportunities with input from your team and show you exactly how to start on each one.
> - If you move ahead with the Roadmap within 30 days, the $497 comes off the price.
> - If the Teardown isn't worth it to you, say so on the call and I'll refund it.
> - This price holds for 14 days after your report.
>
> **[Claim your Teardown]**

Why each piece is there:
- **Found-cost anchor instead of "regularly $1,500."** Cold prospects never pay $1,500 and warm prospects pay nothing, so right now the $1,500 is a price nobody is charged. A skeptic reads "(regularly $1,500)" as marketing-speak. Advertised reference prices generally need to be genuine (worth confirming with a lawyer). The prospect's own dollar figure is a stronger anchor anyway. If JC wants to keep $1,500, make it the real price for direct purchases outside the funnel.
- **Credit toward the Roadmap** answers "I'm paying $497 to be sold to."
- **A simple refund** costs little at this price and removes the risk in a cold purchase.
- **The 14-day window** is real scarcity: a deadline that is actually enforced.

**Warm path:** the teardown note already uses "$1,500 value, free for you." Same fix: "normally $497, free for you," or keep $1,500 only if it becomes a real direct price.

#### 7. Routing gates (the offer qualifies itself)
Offer the Teardown only when all three are true:
- Revenue is $1M or more.
- The Stage 1 estimate clears a threshold (suggested starting point: $15K a year; tune with real data).
- The intent answer isn't "just gathering information."

Everyone else gets the report plus a nurture sequence. A $10K Roadmap pitch to a sub-$1M business, or one with a $3K finding, damages the brand more than it earns.

#### 8. Revised Teardown-to-Roadmap transition

> "Your Teardown found about $[X] a year across [2–3] processes, and it covered [2] of your six business areas. The Roadmap covers all six. We talk to your team, map how work actually moves, and hand you a sequenced plan: what to build first, who owns it, what it costs, and what it returns. [If Blue Tusk implements: it also becomes the build scope, so nothing gets rediscovered.]"

This anchors the $10K against a dollar figure they've already accepted as theirs, and it uses the coverage map as the visual reason to go further.

#### 9. Build scope for launch
Two hooks, two report types, automated competitor discovery, a web view and a PDF are a lot to build before a single real response. Suggested v1:
- One engine and one report containing the cost section plus a light "what peers do" section.
- Two landing pages that differ in copy only.
- Test the hooks first as cold-email subject lines through Instantly.
- Defer automated competitor discovery and the sequential image and subhead tests until traffic can support them. At solo-consultancy volume, those tests won't reach a readable result for months.

**Round 2 objections, raised against the revised version:**
- **"JC reviewing every report doesn't scale."** Absorbed: fine at launch volume, and the review doubles as quality control and lead scoring. Revisit at about 15 a week.
- **"The refund invites gaming."** Absorbed: at $497, with a completed intake as the condition, the risk is small and the conversion gain is worth it.
- **"'Plan 2027' is less urgent than 'close Q4.'"** Absorbed: urgency moves to the per-month cost figure, which is truer and doesn't expire on January 1.
- **"The found-cost anchor backfires when the finding is small."** Refuted by the routing gates: a small finding never sees the offer.
- **"Per-month cost is just the annual figure divided by 12. That's a gimmick."** Absorbed: it's honest, the math is shown, and it's the unit owners actually budget in.

**Round 3:** no new hard objections. The analysis converged.

### Known weaknesses (accepted, not solved)
1. **Proof inventory.** Cold prospects will want evidence, and the vault shows one flagship engagement (Capital Financing). An anonymized sample report helps, but case studies are the real fix. This is the most important unresolved gap.
2. **Knowledge versus progress at the Roadmap level.** The revised Teardown fixes this for Stage 2, but a $10K plan is still a plan. **Open decision for JC:** what happens after the Roadmap? If Blue Tusk implements, say so in the transition. If not, the Roadmap needs its own answer to "then what?"
3. **Launch-quarter timing.** Even retimed, launching in late October means a short runway before the holidays. Accepted: the cost-based urgency carries into Q1 unchanged.
4. **Low traffic makes every test slow.** Accepted; that's why §9 moves the first test to cold email.
5. **The Teardown review call still costs JC calendar time.** This trade-off was accepted in the teardown note; call volume remains a watch item.
